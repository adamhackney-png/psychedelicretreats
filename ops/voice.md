# House voice

How Psychedelic Retreats Australia sounds in guides and destination pages. `ops/compliance-checklist.md`
and `CONTENT.md` win over this file every time: if a sentence reads well but breaks a compliance rule, cut it.

## In one sentence

A calm, well-read friend who has looked into this carefully, tells you plainly what is known and what isn't,
and never tries to sell you anything.

Models: DoubleBlind's practical guides, the NHS and healthdirect for plain writing on sensitive topics,
and Wait But Why's habit of explaining *why* before *what*. Not models: wellness marketing, retreat brochures,
"journey" language.

## What we do

- **Why before what.** Explain the reason behind a rule before the rule. "The Netherlands banned mushrooms in
  2008 but the law's wording missed truffles" makes the Dutch position stick better than "truffles are legal".
- **Name the confusing bit.** Many readers arrive with one misunderstanding (that "legal there" means "safe",
  or "legal to bring home"). Say so plainly, then clear it up.
- **One concrete picture per hard idea.** A short, everyday comparison or a labelled example, where it makes a
  legal or practical point clearer. Never a picture that makes a substance or experience sound appealing.
- **Say what we don't know.** "We haven't found a primary source for this" is a feature of the site. Keep it.
- **Plain words, Australian spelling, AUD.** Short sentences mixed with longer ones. "You" for the reader.
- **"We" is the editorial team** doing real research ("the operators we've reviewed"). Never a named person,
  never invented first-hand experience ("when we attended…").

## What we never do

- Humour, wordplay or a light tone about substances, ceremonies, risk or mental health.
- Efficacy, healing or outcome language, even softened ("can help with", "many find relief"). See compliance
  rules 1, 2 and 11.
- Wellness clichés: journey, transformative, profound, sacred (except in a source's own words, attributed),
  unlock, awaken, embark, medicine (as our own word for a substance).
- Hype or urgency, and anything that reads as encouragement to attend.

## Sentences that read as human

- **Em dashes:** about two per 1,000 words at most. Use a full stop, comma or brackets.
- **No "not X, it's Y" contrasts**, staged openers ("Here's the thing", "The short version"), one-line dramatic
  closers, or habitual lists of three.
- **Inflated words to avoid:** crucial, vital, landscape (figuratively), navigate (figuratively), genuinely,
  meaningfully, materially, comprehensive, robust, delve.
- **No section summaries** that repeat the section.

## Process

1. Follow `CONTENT.md` and the compliance checklist.
2. Write or rewrite in this voice.
3. Keep every fact, source, date, required block and `{/* TODO-ADAM */}` note exactly as it was.
4. Read it as a cautious reader who might be vulnerable: is anything here encouragement, reassurance or a
   claim we can't source? Cut it.
5. `npm run build`.
