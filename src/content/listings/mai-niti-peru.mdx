Mai Niti is a family-run ayahuasca centre in the Shipibo community of San Francisco de Yarinacocha, on the outskirts of Pucallpa in Peru's Ucayali region, about an hour by road from Pucallpa airport. The operator says it was founded in 2011. Ceremonies are led by Maestra Lucila, a Shipibo curandera, together with Maestro Leonardo; the operator describes Leonardo as Lucila's father and teacher, and says Lucila spent three years in isolation following plant dietas before becoming a curandera. Discovery calls and integration support are handled by the head facilitator, Peter Couch, who the site says joined the centre in 2019.

The program linked here is the operator's "1 Week" retreat (8 days, 7 nights), which includes three ayahuasca ceremonies and one San Pedro (wachuma) ceremony, with one-to-one integration support and flexible start dates throughout the year. The operator says doses are introduced gradually: newcomers take a small amount, or none, in the first ceremony while the maestros assess what to serve in later ones, and drinking ayahuasca is optional throughout. Depending on the guest, a stay may also include plant baths, traditional emetics (vomitivos) and master plant dietas, alongside plant walks and Shipibo craft workshops. Groups average about 10 people, with a stated maximum of 15. Each guest has a private bedroom, all meals are provided, and a Spanish–English translator is available at all times, including in ceremony. Longer formats run from five days to several months, including dedicated master plant dieta stays.

Booking starts with a discovery call with the head facilitator, which may be followed by a call with Maestra Lucila. The operator says it checks guests' medications and mental-health history before arrival, lists SSRI, SNRI and MAOI antidepressants and ADHD medication as incompatible with ayahuasca, and publishes a low-tyramine diet to follow before ceremonies. It also says some mental-health conditions (including psychosis, schizophrenia and bipolar disorder with psychotic features) and serious cardiovascular conditions may rule out participation. According to the FAQ, a bilingual facilitator is present in every ceremony and family members are nearby to assist. The operator refers to local medical facilities and transport arrangements, but the site does not publish a written emergency procedure or mention on-site medical staff. The homepage displays the operator's Google reviews and links to its TripAdvisor page.

As reviewed on 25 November 2026, the operator's price table listed $1,250 for the 8-day retreat, $999 for a 6-day, 5-night stay with three ayahuasca ceremonies, $2,100 for two weeks, $2,600 for three weeks and $3,100 per month for stays of up to four months. The price table does not name the currency (the FAQ quotes other costs in US dollars). Prices include the private room, meals, other plant preparations and integration support; kambo is an optional extra charge, and airport transfers from Pucallpa cost extra (the FAQ quotes a taxi fare of 80 soles). Flights to Lima and on to Pucallpa are not included.

This program is held in Peru, where traditional ayahuasca and San Pedro (huachuma) ceremony is legal and culturally protected (see our Peru destination guide). Ayahuasca (which contains DMT) and San Pedro (which contains mescaline) are prohibited substances in Australia. Australians travel at their own responsibility; this is not medical advice.
