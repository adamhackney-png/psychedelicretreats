MycoSpirit runs a 7-day, 6-night residential psilocybin retreat at a private oceanfront villa in the Ocho Rios area of Jamaica, for groups of no more than six participants. The published program includes preparatory sessions before arrival, five guided sessions during the retreat, a daily integration program, and up to three months of integration support after participants return home. Each participant has a private bedroom and bathroom; airport transfers and meals are included, while flights and travel insurance are not. Group dates are confirmed individually after an application is reviewed, and a private one-to-one format is also offered.

Every applicant completes a confidential suitability screening before participation is confirmed. The operator publishes a list of factors that may affect eligibility, including a history of psychosis, schizophrenia or bipolar disorder, seizure disorders, serious cardiovascular conditions, pregnancy or breastfeeding, and certain prescription medications, and it states that participants should not stop or change prescribed medication without speaking to their doctor. The operator describes its program as a retreat and personal-development program, not clinical therapy, medical care or emergency care.

The retreat is led by founder Dmitry Matveev, described on the site as practising since 2016, having guided more than 600 ceremonies, and having led group retreats in Jamaica for the past three years; the site also describes him as a hypnotherapist. The operator is Mycospirit Limited, company number 124383, which the Companies Office of Jamaica register lists as an active local company incorporated on 7 May 2024.

Published pricing is US$5,000 per person for the 7-day group format and US$10,000 for the private program, all-inclusive of the items above (the operator's terms state that prices are in US dollars).

This program is held in Jamaica, where psilocybin is not a prohibited substance. Psilocybin remains a prohibited substance in Australia outside the authorised clinical pathway. Australians travel at their own responsibility; this is not medical advice.
