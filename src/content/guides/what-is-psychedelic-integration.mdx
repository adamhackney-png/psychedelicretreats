"Integration" comes up constantly in this space, often without being clearly defined. This guide covers what it actually means and how to find legitimate support in Australia, including in the weeks after you return from an overseas retreat. It is general information, not medical advice.

## What integration means

Integration is the process of making sense of, and applying insights from, an intense psychological experience — commonly discussed in the context of psychedelic experiences, but the underlying idea (structured reflection and support after something psychologically significant) isn't unique to this context. Practitioners who describe themselves as offering psychedelic integration support are generally providing talk therapy focused specifically on this kind of processing, not administering any substance themselves.

This matters because it means integration support is a fundamentally different — and more broadly and legally accessible — category of support than a retreat or clinical psychedelic-assisted therapy. You don't need a scheduled retreat or a TGA-authorised prescription to access it.

## Why it's considered important

Intense experiences, psychedelic or otherwise, don't automatically translate into durable change on their own — the widely-held view among practitioners in this space is that what you do with an experience afterwards matters as much as the experience itself. This is general information reflecting how practitioners in this space describe their work, not a clinical claim about outcomes.

## Plan support before you travel

Many retreat programs include some virtual integration sessions afterwards, but how many, and with whom, varies by operator (see our guide to [how psilocybin retreats work](/guides/psilocybin-retreats-where-legal-how-they-work/)). Whatever the operator provides, it is worth knowing who you could contact at home before you travel, rather than searching while you are tired and unsettled. Asking an operator what aftercare it provides is a sensible question when you compare programs (see our [vetting checklist](/guides/retreat-vetting-checklist-12-questions/)).

## Finding support in Australia

**Practitioners who offer integration sessions.** Some Australian psychologists and counsellors offer talk-therapy sessions for people processing a psychedelic experience. They do not supply or administer any substance. Several are listed in this directory (see the related listings on this page). For example, one Sydney-based practitioner offers sessions specifically for people integrating experiences undertaken independently, explicitly not a dosing or facilitation service, with published per-session rates. Another, multi-site practitioner offers support across the preparation and integration stages from several Australian locations plus national telehealth.

**Your GP and Medicare.** A GP can talk through how you are going, discuss medications and other health matters, and prepare a mental health treatment plan where appropriate. Under the Better Access initiative, people assessed with a clinically diagnosed mental disorder can claim a Medicare benefit for up to 10 individual and 10 group mental health treatment sessions per calendar year, with eligible psychologists, social workers, occupational therapists and GPs (Department of Health, Disability and Ageing, last updated 6 February 2026). Whether a particular integration practitioner's sessions attract a rebate depends on their profession and registration, so ask them. Tell your GP what you took and when, because it is relevant to their advice. Medicare Mental Health Centres also offer free mental health support with no referral or appointment needed.

**Peer and community groups.** Some community groups run integration circles. Quality and safeguards vary widely, so apply the same questions below.

## Questions to ask before booking

- What are your qualifications, and what is your Ahpra registration number (if you are in a registered profession)?
- What experience do you have with people returning from psychedelic experiences?
- Do you offer any dosing, sourcing or facilitation? (A legitimate integration practitioner should say no.)
- What are your fees, is a Medicare rebate available, and what is your cancellation policy?
- How is my information kept, and what are the limits of confidentiality?

Registered health practitioners appear on the public register kept by Ahpra, which also shows any conditions on their registration. Not every counselling title is a registered profession, so check what the person's title means and ask about their training. Few integration practitioners have an established third-party review presence, so asking for references is reasonable.

## When something feels wrong

Strong emotions, disrupted sleep or a sense of disconnection can follow an intense experience, and they are a good reason to talk to someone, not to wait it out alone. If you are in distress, contact **Lifeline on 13 11 14** (24-hour crisis support), or call **000** if life is in danger. Contact your GP if symptoms persist.

## Legal note

Psilocybin and most other psychedelics are illegal in Australia outside tightly controlled medical pathways (see [is psilocybin legal in Australia?](/guides/is-psilocybin-legal-in-australia-state-by-state/)). Seeking support after a retreat overseas is a health matter: practitioners cannot help you obtain substances, and we do not list anyone who does.
