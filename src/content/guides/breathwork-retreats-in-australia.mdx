Breathwork retreats are the odd one out in this directory. They're legally straightforward, because no controlled substances are involved, but they aren't risk-free, and they're worth understanding on their own terms rather than as a substitute for the substance-based categories on this site.

## What breathwork actually is

"Breathwork" covers a range of specific techniques. Conscious Connected Breathwork is one commonly referenced by the Australian operators we've reviewed. They generally involve a structured, often rapid breathing pattern intended to induce an altered state of consciousness without any substance. Because no substance is involved, none of the jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction legal questions covered elsewhere on this site apply. It's legal to practise and to offer commercially, in Australia and most other countries.

## Why people compare it to psychedelic work

Some practitioners and participants describe breathwork as producing psychologically intense states with some phenomenological similarity to other altered-state practices, which is part of why it gets discussed alongside psychedelic retreats. That's a description of how it's commonly framed, not a claim we're independently verifying about the experience itself.

## What Australian options look like

The two operators we've reviewed for this directory both run retreats in the Byron Bay region of NSW. One offers shorter and multi-day formats combining Conscious Connected Breathwork with practices like Qi Gong and yoga. The other runs a longer (around 6-day) residential immersion that can count towards a breathwork practitioner certification. Neither publishes detailed screening criteria or pricing on the pages we reviewed, so ask about both directly before booking.

## Who should be cautious

Breathwork is not appropriate for everyone. Commonly cited cautions include certain cardiovascular conditions, epilepsy, pregnancy, and a history of psychosis. This is general information, not medical advice. A responsible operator will ask about these before you attend. If they don't, note it.

We include breathwork because it's a legitimate, legal, substance-free option worth knowing about, not because we claim it's equivalent to, or a substitute for, the substance-based categories.
