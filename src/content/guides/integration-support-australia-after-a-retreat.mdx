## Why plan support before you leave

Many retreat programs include some virtual integration sessions afterwards, but how many, and with whom, varies by operator (see our guide to [how psilocybin retreats work](/guides/psilocybin-retreats-where-legal-how-they-work/)). Whatever the operator provides, it is worth knowing who you could contact at home before you travel, rather than searching while you are tired and unsettled. Our [vetting checklist](/guides/retreat-vetting-checklist-12-questions/) includes asking what aftercare is offered.

## Types of support available in Australia

**Practitioners who offer integration sessions.** Some Australian psychologists and counsellors describe offering talk-therapy sessions for people processing a psychedelic experience. They do not supply or administer any substance. Several are listed in this directory: see the related listings on this page.

**Your GP.** A GP can talk through how you are going, discuss medications and other health matters, and prepare a referral or mental health plan where appropriate. Tell them what you took and when, because it is relevant to their advice. They are a medical professional, not a legal one, and you can ask about confidentiality.

**Peer and community groups.** Some community groups run integration circles. Quality and safeguards vary widely, so apply the same questions below.

## Questions to ask before booking

- What are your qualifications, and what is your Ahpra registration number (if you are a registered profession)?
- What experience do you have with people returning from psychedelic experiences?
- Do you offer any dosing, sourcing or facilitation? (A legitimate integration practitioner should say no.)
- What are your fees, and what is your cancellation policy?
- How is my information kept, and what are the limits of confidentiality?

## Checking registration

Registered health practitioners in Australia appear on the public register kept by Ahpra, which also shows any conditions on their registration. Not every counselling title is a registered profession, so check what the person's title means and ask about their training.

## When something feels wrong

Strong emotions, disrupted sleep or a sense of disconnection can follow an intense experience, and they are a good reason to talk to someone, not to wait it out alone. If you are in distress, contact **Lifeline on 13 11 14** (24-hour crisis support), or call **000** if life is in danger. Contact your GP if symptoms persist.

## Legal note

Psilocybin and most other psychedelics are illegal in Australia outside tightly controlled medical pathways. Seeking support after a retreat overseas is a health matter: practitioners cannot help you obtain substances, and we do not list anyone who does. Australians travel at their own responsibility; this is not medical advice.
