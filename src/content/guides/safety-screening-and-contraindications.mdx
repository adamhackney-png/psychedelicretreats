This guide is informational, not medical advice. It describes patterns we've seen among the better-documented operators in this directory. It isn't a personal risk assessment, which only a qualified health professional can give you.

## Commonly cited contraindications

Across the operators whose screening materials we've reviewed for this directory, certain conditions and circumstances come up again and again as reasons for exclusion, or as needing specific medical sign-off:

- a history of psychosis or certain psychotic-spectrum diagnoses
- active suicidal ideation
- certain dissociative or personality disorders
- recent psychiatric hospitalisation
- pregnancy
- interactions with specific medication classes (SSRIs and MAOIs are commonly flagged for ayahuasca specifically, given known interaction risks).

This describes what we've seen operators publish. It isn't an exhaustive medical list, and it can't tell you whether something applies to you. Contraindications vary by substance, by individual health history, and by current medication.

## What a real screening process looks like

The strongest example we found while researching this directory is worth describing concretely. A Peru-based ayahuasca centre's safety materials describe a screening process developed with a harm-reduction organisation and reviewed by an off-site doctor and psychologist, and the centre states that roughly 70% of applicants are ultimately accepted. In other words, about three in ten applicants aren't accepted, which suggests the screening is doing real work rather than being a formality. Compare that with an operator that only says "we screen participants" with no further detail. The difference in what you can actually check is large, and that's what our vetting checklist tries to bring out.

## What a real emergency protocol looks like

The strongest emergency-protocol disclosure we found is similarly specific: an onsite nurse or medical professional, named emergency equipment (one operator specifically mentioned an AED), a named contracted clinic or hospital, and a stated evacuation time and method. Weaker disclosure just says "trained staff on hand" without any of these details. That sounds reassuring, but it's a much weaker claim.

## Independent verification matters

Safety information published by an operator is self-reported by definition. That's why our vetting checklist labels items "stated" rather than "verified", and why we suggest checking independent reviews (not just testimonials curated on the operator's own site) as a separate signal. See our 12-question vetting guide for the fuller list of questions worth asking directly.

## Mental health support

If anything in this guide, or in your own circumstances, raises concerns about your mental health, Lifeline (13 11 14) is available 24/7, and in an emergency call 000. This guide, and this site generally, is not a substitute for professional mental health support.
